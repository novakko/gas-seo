It has been just over 2 years since I published the last Local Search Ranking Factors report. Since then, the SEO industry has experienced significant shifts in our understanding and approach to SEO, driven by the growth of large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT and Gemini for informational queries.

Local search has remained somewhat insulated from this AI upheaval. While informational content-based websites have seen their traffic fall off a cliff with the introduction of AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, and others, research and discovery of local businesses is still predominantly done through Google’s local results because it’s a much better experience for local intent queries than LLM responses.

Understanding what drives local rankings and how to improve them remains incredibly valuable for businesses and agencies alike.

This is not to say there haven’t been changes in local search, though. There have been significant changes. Read on to learn what’s new.

47 of the best and the brightest local search experts are invited to complete a comprehensive 2-hour survey, weighing and scoring 187 factors and answering open-ended questions about what they see driving local search rankings today. Each factor is scored between 0 and 5 across the following areas:

  1. Local pack/Maps ranking impact
  2. Local organic ranking impact
  3. Conversion impact
  4. AI Search visibility impact (new for 2026)

The combined results from all their scores are summed and ranked by total score to determine which factors the experts believe have the greatest impact and which have little to no impact (local SEO myths).

None of the experts have special access to the internal workings of Google’s local search algorithm. The factor list and the responses are based on over a decade of analysis, experience, research, and testing the specific signals Google uses to evaluate and rank businesses.

That said, the factors at the top of the lists unquestionably have the most significant impact on rankings, based on the observations of the local search experts who optimize businesses to rank in local results.

Local Search Ranking Factor groups

Individual factors are organized into the following groups:

Google Business Profile signals

Proximity, categories, keywords in business name, etc.

On-page signals

Presence of NAP, keywords in title tag, domain authority, etc.

Review signals

First & third party reviews, review quantity, review velocity, etc.

Link signals

Inbound anchor text, linking domain authority, linking domain quantity, etc.

Behavioural signals

Click-through rate, mobile clicks to call, dwell time, etc.

Citation signals

Location data, NAP consistency, citation volume, etc.

Personalization signals

Search history, search location, device, etc.

Social signals

Follower count, engagement, quality of posts, etc.

Observations

Noteworthy new factors

Business is Open at Time of Search (Business Hours) - #5

In December of 2023, Joy Hawkins dropped a bomb on me and the rest of the local search community when she revealed on a webinar that there was a new local ranking factor. Whether your business is open or closed at the time of the search will have a huge impact on your rankings. You can watch me go through the full range emotions on the recording:

  1. Skepticism
  2. Disbelief
  3. Grief (because we operate a local tracker and would have to update it to deal with this)
  4. Acceptance

So, of course I had to add this new factor to survey, and it has already skyrocketed to position #5 in terms of ranking impact. That position feels right to me. You can watch it happen in real time. When your business is open you can be sitting pretty at #1 in the local results, and then 60 minutes before you close you’ll start to drop off, and then when you’re actually closed you’ll be displaced by businesses that are open.

Google wants to show people businesses that are open. This makes sense in some industries like retail or restaurants, but it doesn’t make sense in others like home services or lawyers. What if someone is researching lawyers late at night? Your business is invisible because you’re closed? That’s frustrating for the business and honestly, not a good experience for the user.

Some businesses are extending their hours to get some bonus ranking time, and others are paying for call answering services so they can set their GBPs to be open 24/7. This is fine for appointment-only businesses, but do NOT list your hours other than what they actually are for any brick and mortar businesses where people might actually show up during your stated opening hours. That will frustrate customers and could lead to negative reviews, and it’s also against Google’s guidelines.

Address Is Showing on GBP (Not SAB) - #7

This is a contentious one for me. Based on the scores this factor received, everyone seems to be in agreement that if you remove your address from your Google Business Profile, you’ll lose all your rankings. And I get it. I’ve seen it happen. So, showing your address would be an important ranking factor, right?

But here’s the thing. I’ve seen a couple of cases where the address was removed and rankings did not budge. They stayed exactly the same as they were before.

So what gives? Why do some businesses lose all their rankings when they hide their address and others do not?

I don’t know for sure yet, but I plan to get to the bottom of this mystery (it’s on my long list of “plans”).

I currently have 2 theories:

  1. If a Google Business Profile was originally registered at one address, then they later moved, then they had to remove the address for guidelines compliance, then it will look like they lost all their rankings. But what actually happened is that their rankings just moved back to the previous address. I’ve seen this exact scenario happen a couple of times. When there is no address on the GBP anymore, Google has to set the pin of the business at the only address they have, which is the address the GBP was verified at.

  2. I am also aware of cases where the business had to remove their address, their rankings disappeared, and they are absolutely adamant that the address they had on the profile is the same as the one they verified at. So we can’t explain these cases with “they moved”. Something else is going on, and I think it’s a bug with Google.

    I think that sometimes (not always) when you remove an address from a profile, Google loses the geo coordinates of the location and just makes a best guess of where to put the business (potentially related to the service areas that are set?). I saw a case recently where Google dropped the business 200 miles north in the middle of the desert. The scorpions out there weren’t searching for his car detailing service, so his calls dropped to zero.

    How did I figure out that Google moved his pin 200 miles north? I have a cool trick where I set up a massive grid using our new Local Ranking Grids software that covers the whole state. When I see a hint of green, that gives me an idea of the general location that Google has put the business, and then I can create more focused grids to “zoom in” on the location. Neat, eh?

Anyway, I remain unconvinced that showing your address vs hiding your address is a ranking factor. I think it’s a bug. In all the cases I have looked at, rankings didn’t disappear. They just moved somewhere else. They moved back to the previous address, or, they moved to some totally random whacky place.

Apparently I’m the only one who thinks this, though. I will get back to you when I do more research.

Presence of GBP Predefined Services - #22

Right after I published the 2023 Local Search Ranking Factors, Joy Hawkins released some new research showing that the predefined Services that Google suggests on your Google Business Profile have an impact on rankings. This was super cool (not) because services were listed so low on the factor list (#81) as to almost be considered a local search myth in the 2023 survey 🤦🏻‍♂️

So yeah, I added a new factor for “Presence of GBP Predefined Services” this year, and wouldn’t you know it, it scored very highly on the list as the #22 most impactful ranking factor.

I have confirmed this with my own testing. Yep, services impact rankings.

Noteworthy factors that increased in importance

As mentioned above, we now know that the Services section on your impact rankings, so it’s no surprise that this factor increased 75 positions from the bottom of the list at 110 up to 35.

64 - Proper Hours Set on GBP

Also noted above, the hours you set on your Profile can significantly impact your rankings, so the expert contributors are now scoring this factor much higher than in previous years. It has increased 64 positions from 85 to 21.

32 - In-store visits tracked by Android or Google Maps Mobile app location detection

Um… what? Haha. I don’t know what’s going on with this factor. Apparently, the experts think this is more important than it used to be. It moved up from 73 to 41. I suppose it’s conceivable that Google has gotten better at tracking locations via people’s mobile devices, and they’re putting increased importance on foot traffic as a ranking signal. Sure, I accept this premise.

14 - Quantity of Engagement Signals on GBP

This factor increased from 48 to 34, and I dig it. I think Google is leaning heavily on user behaviour signals, and real humans engaging with your GBP — by dwelling on it, looking through your photos, reading reviews, watching videos, and reading your posts — will definitely help you improve your rankings. (emdashes added by me, not ChatGPT!)

This is yet another reason to build out your Profile and update it regularly. Give people content to engage with, and the algorithm will reward you. But even better than the algorithmic benefits: you’ll drive more conversions.

Noteworthy factors that decreased in importance

65 - Internal Links FROM GBP Landing Page to Other Pages of Website

Take a moment to think about the page you link to on your GBP. Usually, this is the homepage, but it can sometimes be a dedicated location page for multi-location businesses. It’s important to have well-structured, optimized content on this page that reflects the topics you want your GBP to rank for. This page needs to have lots of links TO the page, both external and internal. Does it matter much what other pages you link to FROM this page? Not that much, so this is why we’re seeing this factor drop in importance from #45 to #110. Makes sense.

64 - Linking Out to Authority Topically Relevant Sites

There is a common(ish) recommendation to link out to authority sites from your key landing pages. For example, a page about hot water tank repair might include a link to the US Department of Energy’s Hot Water Heaters page.

Is this important to do and will it increase your rankings? Apparently, the local search experts think this is less important than it used to be, because it dropped 64 spots. But this factor wasn’t highly ranked before anyway. It was ranked 113 in 2023, and now it’s ranked WAY down the list at 177.

40 - Volume of Quality Content on Service Pages

You know those recipe blog posts with 10,000 words about the history of their grandma’s apple pie? Don’t do that. People just want the recipe.

It’s 2026, and massive pages of content are no longer the way. The new way is to write clear, concise, quality content that answers the user’s questions with no fluff. Give them the recipe right at the start.

This will help you in AI search as well. Just like humans, the robots also don’t want to read your drivel.

Citations are back!

Citations as a local search strategy have been decreasing in importance for the past decade, but AI SEO is breathing new life into them. 3 of the top 5 AI Search visibility factors are citation factors.

In AI SEO, mentions (citations) are the new link.

How to rank in Local Services Ads

In categories where Local Services Ads exist, they dominate the top of the search results and steal a large share of clicks from the local pack and organic results. Google has done a great job of making these results look just as compelling as the local pack. We have personal injury clients who rank extremely well in both local and organic results, but still drive 70% of their cases through Local Services Ads.

If you have a business in a competitive industry where Google shows Local Services Ads, you should get up to speed on how to set up and optimize these ad accounts. Fortunately, many of the local search experts have been optimizing, testing, and discovering what works to improve placement in these ad packs.

The top 4 factors are:

  1. Budget & Bidding
  2. Reviews
  3. Service Selection
  4. Response Times

I can speak from personal experience and add some nuance to “Reviews”. Yes, rating and number of reviews are important, but just like with local pack rankings, Review Recency seems to be a significant factor for our clients to improve LSA visibility.

See the rest of the LSA ranking factors.

“Length of title tag” is an SEO myth

I added a new factor to the survey for “Length of Title Tag” and was pleased to see that it scored near the very bottom of the factor list. Low enough to call it a myth.

Did all of you at the back of the room catch that? TITLE TAG LENGTH IS AN SEO MYTH. You do not need to worry about making sure your title tags are 60 characters. That’s a made up recommendation from SEO tools based on how many characters display in the search results. Google doesn’t care about how long your titles are, though. They will use long titles and all those extra terms you put in them will help you rank.

Personally, I love to stuff the back end of my title tags with related terms I want the page to rank for. It has improved rankings every time. And I kind of appreciate how Google truncates them in the search results. I put all the nice-looking click-worthy text at the front to compel the click, and add my bonus keywords to the back end, where they won’t get displayed in the SERPs anyway.

The future of local search

Have you tried Google’s AI Mode? I think that’s a glimpse into what the future of local search will look like. The traditional search results will be replaced with AI results. We’re seeing them test the waters right now with AI Overviews. They just have to figure out how to integrate ads so they keep that business growing.

What does this mean for your SEO strategy? Fortunately, if you were doing good SEO before, then you should not need to change much. In addition to all the standard GBP and website optimization work you are doing (as guided by this resource), here are the “new” things I’m recommending, if you’re not already doing them:

  1. Get listed on expert-curated "best of" lists. This is easier said than done. You have to petition these sites and ask them to include your brand, but these lists try to be unbiased, so good luck.
  2. Get listed on all the prominent directory sites in your industry. (Tip: you can ask ChatGPT and Gemini which sites they think are important)
    1. Optimize those listings by filling out every field
    2. Ensure you've optimized the description with a good semantic triple in the first sentence.
  3. Get reviews on the prominent review sites in your industry. You can't just send review requests to Google anymore.
  4. Make sure you have a testimonials/reviews page on your own site, and it's linked in the main nav
  5. Work on your website content, making sure it's:
    1. Answering the important questions people want to know about the topic (use AlsoAsked.com)
    2. Well structured semantically (H1, H2s, H3s, bullets. No walls of texts)
    3. Your content should be concise, punchy, confident, and factual. Avoid extra flourish.
  6. Get local mentions:
    1. Do some local community engagement
    2. Consider doing op-eds for smaller local newspapers.
    3. Collab with local bloggers
